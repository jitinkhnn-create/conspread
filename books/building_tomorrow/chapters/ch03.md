A. I am often unable to express myself. How shall I?
Q. I don’t know how to express myself; maybe others make
fun of me, etc.- thoughts come to my mind. There is no harm
or need to express myself, as life still moves on.
Expressing ourselves is very important. It helps others
understand how we feel and what we think. When we share
our thoughts and feelings, we can make friends and solve
problems. Sometimes, we might worry that people will laugh
at us or not accept us.
Remember, everyone feels this way sometimes. Be brave and
speak up! If someone makes fun of you, stay calm. They might
not understand you yet. Keep expressing yourself kindly and
confidently. Over time, people will respect and appreciate your
honesty. Always remember, your thoughts and feelings are
important and deserve to be heard.
Remember always: Life is on the other side of fear.
Q. I don’t ask questions as I feel like I would sound stupid or
that my question would be irrelevant.
Never be afraid to ask questions! Asking questions is how we
learn and grow. Even adults ask questions when they don't
know something. No question is stupid or irrelevant. If you
don't understand something, it's likely others don't either.
When you ask, you help everyone learn. If someone makes you
feel bad for asking, remember that everyone was a beginner

[[p.54]]
once. Stay curious and keep asking questions. It's a smart and
brave thing to do, and it helps you become wiser every day.
Q. We have faced many times a situation where we are unable
to express ourselves and get into a mess. But what is the
solution? The other person doesn’t understand me well, and I
am not interested in sharing myself either.
Sometimes it’s hard to know what to say. Here are some tips
to help you choose the right words:
1.
Stay Calm: Don’t panic when explaining your situation. Take
a deep breath.
2.
Be Honest: Use words that exactly match your feelings.
It’s okay to say how you truly feel.
3.
Learn New Words: Try to increase your vocabulary about
feelings and situations. This helps you express yourself
better.
4.
Tell the Whole Story: Don’t just share your side. Explain
the complete situation so others understand.
5.
Be Patient: Don’t expect immediate solutions. Sometimes,
just sharing is enough.
By following these tips, you can express yourself clearly and
confidently.
Q. Why do I feel negative, despite having so much in life, and
when I do feel grateful for things in life at times?
Even when we have many good things in life and feel grateful,
we can still sometimes feel negative. Here are some reasons
why this might happen:
• Pressure and Expectations: Sometimes, we feel a lot of
pressure to do well in school, sports, or other activities.

[[p.55]]
• Comparison: We might compare ourselves to others and
feel we are not good enough, even if we feel grateful for
the resources in life.
• Loneliness: Having everything doesn’t always mean having
friends or feeling understood. Feeling lonely can make us
feel negative.
• Changes and Uncertainty: Growing up involves many
changes and uncertainties, which can be scary and make
us feel uneasy or negative.
• Emotional Growth: We are still learning to understand and
manage our emotions. Sometimes, we might feel sad or
negative without a clear reason.
• Mental Health: Just like adults, we experience mental
health issues like anxiety or depression, which can make
us feel negative even when things seem good.
How To Cope With Negative Feelings
• Talk About It: Encourage yourself to talk about your
feelings with a trusted adult or friend. Sharing can help
you feel better and understood.
• Practice Mindfulness: Be mindful of your feelings and
practice deep breathing or relaxation techniques when you
feel overwhelmed.
• Focus on Positives: Focus on positive experiences and
achievements, no matter how small.
• Stay Active: Physical activity can improve mood and
reduce negative feelings. Encourage yourself to play
outside, join a sport, or take walks.
It's important to understand that feeling negative sometimes
is a normal part of life. Acknowledging these feelings and
learning how to manage them is key to emotional well-being.

Q. Why does it take a long time to recover from my upset
mood?
-Sometimes, when you feel sad or upset, it can take you a
whole week to feel better because your feelings are big and
hard to understand. You might think about what happened
over and over again, and it feels like you can’t shake off the
sadness. This means you are thinking about the past rather
than living in the present moment, when that problem does
not exist, yet you are dragging it into the present.
It’s a matter of your choice either to carry pain in the future
or to live happily and freely.
We might not know how to talk about our feelings or feel
scared to share ourselves. It's ok to be wrong; it's ok to lose.
What matters is not your way of looking at the situation. It’s
a matter of how much you trust yourself. Believe in yourself,
work to the best of your ability, and with this, you will be
attentive and positive.
To help you feel better faster, it would be great if you could
talk to someone you trust, like a parent or teacher, about
what’s bothering you.
Doing fun activities that you enjoy, like playing outside or
drawing, can also help you forget about your worries. It’s
important to learn how to calm down, such as by taking deep
breaths or counting to 10. Write down what you are feeling on
a piece of paper or record it on a voice recorder. Moving out of
that situation is important.
Q. I do have a set of irritating friends and people around me.
How shall I deal with them?
Don’t fall for what others say to you; it's just their way of
looking at things. Everyone carries their own perspective on
life. You are who you feel you are. You should not change your
sense of who you are based on how others feel about you. All

[[p.57]]
people are equally important in our lives. We feel irritated
because we think we should get to hear what we like, feel
that it's right, or that we are good. We are not comfortable
with what we are not good at. You may leave the place where
you feel irritated.
But these would not help you learn how to deal, would not
ease your life, and would make you hide when something hits
back at you. Nobody wants to do wrong to another
intentionally, but sometimes they act in ways or take steps
that unknowingly hurt us.
What hurt you is what you took into your mind, and that does
not match our way of thinking. Let others think as they wish
to; they are here to show you another way of looking at things.
You have to listen to others about what they are saying and
why they are saying it. Do not focus on the words you hear
from them; instead, understand why they think what they
think and what makes them believe what they believe about
you.
Others’ points about us are derived from the information
available to them about us, which is incomplete and
assumption-based. So they don’t understand us and could be
on the wrong path. So help them understand who you are and
guide them to the right things. They would not be that
irrational since that day.
Your inner voice, you say, surely says, “Why should I make this
much effort?” “He should understand what is right or what he
should be doing.” These reasons really stop us from moving in
the right direction. These little thoughts aim to provide us
with short-term calm. This fuels our ego and provides no help.
And we shall not hide from such people throughout our lives.
It's better to prepare for and take the exam than to wish for
it not to happen, and to run in the right direction to equip
yourself for a bright future.
