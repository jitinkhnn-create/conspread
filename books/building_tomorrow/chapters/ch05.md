Is It Really True???

Sometimes, what we think is true is only one way of looking
at a situation.
Many times we have been upset with a friend, only to discover
later that there was a misunderstanding. Have you ever
thought a teacher was being unfair, and then realized they
were trying to help? Or have you ever looked at a difficult
challenge and thought,
"I can't do this."
Only to surprise yourself later?
The truth is:
We do not see the world exactly as it is.
As we learned earlier, we see the world through our
experiences, feelings, beliefs, and memories. And because
everyone has different experiences, everyone sees things a
little differently.
That doesn't mean someone is right and someone is wrong.
It simply means there may be more than one way to
understand the same situation.

The Elephant Puzzle
Imagine five people standing around a giant elephant.
One person touches its leg.
"It's a tree!" they say.
Another touches its trunk.
"No, it's a snake!"
A third touches its ear.
"You're both wrong. It's a giant fan!"
Who is correct?
The answer is: all of them are seeing part of the truth.
But none of them is seeing the whole elephant.
Life is often like that.
When we only look from one angle, we may miss important
pieces of the picture.
The more perspectives we consider, the clearer our
understanding becomes.

Changing the Lens
Our perspective is like a pair of glasses.
Different glasses show different pictures.
Let's see how changing our perspective can change our
experience.
School
×
"School is boring and pointless."

"School helps me discover things I don't know yet."
Friends
×
"My friends are the only people who understand me."

"My friends are important, but my family, teachers, and
other trusted adults can help me too."
Challenges
×
"If something feels difficult, I must not be good at it."

"Difficult things often help me grow stronger."
Mistakes
×
"I made a mistake. I failed."

"I made a mistake. I learned something."
Feedback
×
"Someone corrected me because they don't like me."

"Someone corrected me because they noticed something
I can improve."
Mirror Time
Think about a recent situation that upset you.
Now ask yourself:
 Is there another way to look at this?
 What might the other person be thinking or feeling?
 What facts do I know for sure?
 What am I assuming?

[[p.80]]

If my best friend were in this situation, what advice
would I give them?
Write your answers in your journal.
The Perspective Superpower
People who can see situations from different angles often:
 Solve problems better
 Understand others more easily
 Make wiser decisions
 Stay calmer during challenges

Learn faster from mistakes
Seeing different perspectives doesn't mean changing who you
are. It means being curious enough to ask:
"What else could be true?"
That simple question can open doors to understanding,
growth, and better decisions.
Sometimes the biggest change in life doesn't happen when
the situation changes.
Sometimes it happens when our perspective changes.
