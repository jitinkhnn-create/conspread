Support System In Life

Imagine a sports team preparing for an important match.
Most people notice the players on the field, but behind every
successful
team
are
coaches,
trainers,
supporters,
groundskeepers, and many others working quietly in the
background. Life is very similar. Behind every person you
admire stands an invisible team that has helped shape their
journey.
Some offered support, some provided opportunities, some gave
advice, and some taught valuable lessons through challenges.
Understanding this invisible team helps us appreciate how
connected our lives really are.
We are all meant to take action, and our surroundings are our
resources in life for achieving a bigger goal.
Others do carry a perspective for us, and we base our
discussions on seeing others through that understanding &
set of parameters only.
Sometimes we make quick judgments about people based on
how they look, what they wear, or what we’ve heard about
them. This is like looking at them through a lens that can
change how we see them”.
We assume and make a story we believe in
Example:
Parent: Imagine you’re meeting a new student in your class.
What if you decided they weren’t friendly just because they

[[p.100]]
were quiet on the first day? How might that stop you from
making a new friend?
Child: I guess I wouldn’t get to know them.
Parent: Exactly. Sometimes we see people through a lens of
our own ideas and experiences. But if we drop that lens and
really try to get to know them, we can see the real person.
It’s like when you thought your new soccer coach was strict
at first, but then you found out they were really nice and
helpful.
Child: Oh, yeah. I see what you mean.
Parent: So next time, let’s try to learn more about people
before deciding what they’re like. Everyone has a story, just
like you.
All the people around me in this life, apart from ME, are part
of the resources required to live well.
• Someone who listens to you: They help you express
yourself and let you be as you want to be.
• Someone who doesn’t listen to you: He is to create a place
for someone in your life where you know what kind of
person you want to live with and express yourself freely.
• Those who stop you from doing a task: They are sent to
bring out your creativity, to ignite you to create your own
path, and to develop patience in you. Now, creating such a
path, or choosing to feel like a victim, is your choice, and
your life is as you choose it to be.
• Those who raise us: They raise us by teaching us culture
and values during our developmental years. They teach us
things at various stages of our lives. We tale/make
decisions based on our values, and we see things as right
or wrong based on our cultural acceptance of them.

[[p.101]]
• Those who criticize you: They take you to see the other
way possible; they are sent to you to see a new perspective
which you would not otherwise know or wish to see.
• Those who give you a chance: They believe in your ability
and trust you to do what you are capable of. They give you
a ground to play and showcase your potential.
We need to accept all those around us, as they are all part of
one big story meant to grow you and help you grow.

Gratitude: Seeing What We Already Have
Yes, by looking at things positively. Practice Gratitude.
We naturally notice what is missing, what is difficult, and
what still needs improvement. While this can help us grow, it
can also make us overlook the good things already present in

[[p.106]]
our lives. Gratitude helps us shift our attention toward the
people, opportunities, experiences, and resources that support
us every day. It reminds us that even during challenging times,
there are reasons to appreciate the journey and the people
walking alongside us. Let’s enter the world of Gratitude and
make this part of our lives.
How to Express Gratitude
1.
Say Thank You: Verbally express thanks whenever
someone does something kind or helpful. For example,
thank you for helping me with my homework, Mom.
2.
Write a Note: Create thank-you notes or cards for
teachers, friends, or family members. A simple note like
"Thank you for being a great friend" can go a long way.
3.
Show Appreciation Through Actions: Do something nice in
return, such as helping with chores or sharing a toy.
4.
Keep a Gratitude Journal: Write down things you are
thankful for each day. This can be a few sentences or
even just a list.
5.
Publicly Acknowledge: Share gratitude in front of others,
such as during family dinners or class discussions.
When to Express Gratitude
• Daily: Make it a habit to find at least one thing to be
grateful for each day.
• After Receiving Help: Immediately thank someone after
they have helped you.
• Special Occasions: During birthdays, holidays, or special
events, thank people for their gifts and presence.
• Random Acts of Kindness: When someone does something
unexpectedly nice, acknowledge their effort.

[[p.107]]
• Reflective Moments: At the end of the day, reflect on and
express gratitude for the positive experiences.
Making Gratitude a Habit
1.
Start Small: Begin by writing down three things you're
grateful for each day.
2.
Create Reminders: Set reminders on your phone or have
a visual cue, like a gratitude jar, where you can drop in
notes of thanks.
3.
Involve the Family: Have regular family discussions where
everyone shares what they are grateful for.
4.
Practice Mindfulness: Spend a few minutes each day
reflecting on positive experiences and expressing
gratitude for them.
5.
Lead by Example: Watch and learn from how parents,
teachers, and friends show gratitude, and try to emulate
them.
Life with Gratitude
When Practicing Gratitude
1.
Increased Happiness: Regularly focusing on positive aspects
of life can boost overall happiness and satisfaction.
2.
Better Relationships: Expressing gratitude strengthens
bonds with family, friends, and peers. People feel valued
and appreciated.
3.
Improved Mental Health: Gratitude can reduce feelings of
stress, anxiety, and depression by shifting focus away
from negative thoughts.
4.
Enhanced Resilience: Grateful individuals are better
equipped to cope with challenges and setbacks.

[[p.108]]
5.
Greater Empathy: Regularly practicing gratitude fosters a
sense of empathy and compassion towards others.
6.
Positive Outlook: Cultivating a habit of gratitude helps
maintain a positive outlook on life, even during tough
times.
When Not Practicing Gratitude
1.
Increased Negativity: A lack of gratitude can lead to
focusing on what's wrong in life rather than what's right,
fostering a negative mindset.
2.
Strained Relationships: Not expressing gratitude can
make others feel unappreciated, potentially leading to
conflicts or weakened relationships.
3.
Poor Mental Health: Focusing on negative aspects can
increase stress, anxiety, and feelings of depression.
4.
Decreased Resilience: Without gratitude, it may be harder
to cope with life's challenges and setbacks.
5.
Self-Centered Attitude: A lack of gratitude can foster a
sense of entitlement and selfishness, reducing empathy
and compassion for others.
6.
Lower Life Satisfaction: Overall life satisfaction may
decrease as the focus remains on unmet desires and
frustrations rather than on appreciating what one already
has.

Steps To Form A Gratitude Habit
1.
Set a Regular Time: Choose a time each day to reflect on
and express gratitude, such as before bed or during
dinner.
2.
Share Gratitude: Share what you're thankful for with
family or friends regularly.
3.
Model Behavior: Watch how others show gratitude and
follow their example.
4.
Keep It Simple: Gratitude doesn’t need to be grand.
Simple, sincere thanks go a long way.
5.
Stay Consistent: Consistency is key to forming any habit.
Stick with your gratitude practice even on tough days.

How To Manage Expectations
• Be Realistic: Understand that people may respond
differently to gratitude. Some may be very appreciative,
while others might not react as expected.
Example Talk: Sometimes people might not show how
much they appreciate you, but it still makes a difference.
Keep expressing gratitude because it's important for you
and them.
• Respect Differences: Everyone has their own way of
showing and receiving gratitude. What matters is the
genuine intention behind it.
Example Talk: Not everyone shows their feelings the
same way, and that's okay. Your sincere thanks will still
be valued.

Communication: Building Bridges With Others
No matter how much people care about us, they cannot read
our minds. Many misunderstandings arise when thoughts and
feelings go unspoken. Communication helps us express our
needs, share our concerns, solve problems, and strengthen
relationships. Like any skill, communication improves with
practice. Learning to speak honestly, listen carefully, and
respect different viewpoints helps us build stronger
connections with the people around us.
There are some tips for improving this:
Reasons could be:
•
Low self-confidence
•
Not finding the right way to say things
•
Self-voice stops from taking a step
• Non-Fulfillment of expectations from others in terms of
answers
Some thoughts on sharing our thoughts
Q1 Others also don’t share themselves:
• What is really required is our will to move ahead and talk
because sharing is more than fearing for the outcome or
else.
• Living another's way of life is equal to cheating yourself.
• We have to find a solution to our problems; they are not
you, and you are not they.
• Only when you are free mentally can you create space for
new things.
• Life is only available once you cross your fear.
• Communication is a tool required to live life.

[[p.112]]
Q2. They won’t understand me:
Whether this is true or false would depend on what you do in
the conversation.
They might not live up to your resolution, as you might be
looking for an immediate solution/not willing to take
corrective action/etc. - We need to understand that elders
have a different way of looking at things, so they think
differently.
What to do then: - Put your point and understand their
answers/solutions on that. If you do not agree or are not
comfortable, discuss it again. Don’t look for your answers only
because other solutions also exist; they might be a bit far
from our minds.
What is the crux? There are short-term resolutions and long-term solutions to our problems. We shall find long-term
solutions to our problems.
What does it really take to do communication?
1.
Be Honest: Clearly express your feelings without
exaggeration.
2.
Stay Calm: Take deep breaths and try to stay calm when
explaining how you feel.
3.
Be Specific: Explain exactly what is bothering you and
why.
4.
Use I Statements: Focus on your own feelings rather than
blaming others (e.g., I feel... instead of You made me
feel...).
5.
Listen: Be open to the other person's perspective and
listen to their response.
6.
Ask for What You Need: Clearly state what you need or
how others can help you.

[[p.113]]
7.
Be Patient: Understand that others may need time to
respond or may not immediately know how to help.
8.
Respect Differences: Accept that others may have
different viewpoints and solutions.
Some Conversations can be done as follows: Examples
Conversation 1: Child with Parent
•
Situation: Child is feeling overwhelmed with homework.
Child: Mom, I feel really overwhelmed with my homework
today. It's so much, and I don't know where to start.
Parent: I understand; it can be tough sometimes. Can you
show me what you have to do? Let's break it down into
smaller tasks together.
Child: Okay, I have math, science, and English
assignments. I feel like I can't finish them all.
Parent: Let's tackle one subject at a time. How about we
start with math, spend 30 minutes on it, and then take
a short break?
Child: That sounds better. I think I can manage that.
Conversation 2: Child with Teacher
•
Situation: Child is confused about a class lesson.
Child: Mam, I'm having trouble understanding today's
lesson on fractions. Could you explain it to me again?
Teacher: Sure, why not? Which part are you finding most
confusing?
Child: I don't understand how to add fractions with
different denominators.
Teacher: Let's go over it step by step. Remember, you
need to find a common denominator first. Let's work
through a problem together.

[[p.114]]
Conversation 3: Child with Sibling
•
Situation: Child feels left out during playtime.
Child: Hey, can I talk to you for a minute? I feel like I'm
being left out when you play with your friends.
Sibling: I'm sorry, I didn't realize you felt that way. Why
don't you join us? What do you want to play?
Child: I'd like to play tag with you all. Can we do that?
Sibling: Sure, I'll let everyone know. Thanks for telling me
how you feel.
Conversation 4: Child with Friend
•
Situation: Child is feeling sad because of a disagreement
with a friend.
Child: Hey, can we talk? I felt really upset after our
argument yesterday.
Friend: Yeah, I'm sorry too. I didn't mean to hurt feelings.
Child: I want us to understand each other better. I felt
like you weren't listening to me.
Friend: I understand. Let's make sure we both listen to
each other next time. Friends?
Child: Friends.
Conversation 5: Child with Parent (Different Scenario)
•
Situation: Child feels anxious about an upcoming test.
Child: Dad, I'm really nervous about my test tomorrow.
I'm afraid I might fail.
Parent: It's normal to feel nervous before a test. Have you
studied and prepared well?
Child: I think so, but I still feel scared.

[[p.115]]
Parent: Let's review some of the material together.
Sometimes, going over it one more time can help you feel
more confident. Also, remember to take deep breaths and
stay calm.
Conversation 6: Child with Parent (Emotional Support)
•
Situation: Child feels sad and doesn't know why.
Child: Mom, I feel really sad today, but I don't know why.
Parent: It's okay to feel sad sometimes, even if you don't
know why. Do you want to talk about anything that might
be bothering you, or do you need a hug?
Child: I think I need a hug.
Parent: Come here. Remember, it's okay to have days like
this. If you want to talk more later, I'm here for you.
Conversation 7: Child with Teacher (Asking for Help)
•
Situation: Child needs help with a project.
Child: Ma'am, I need some help with my science project.
I'm not sure how to start.
Teacher: Ok. What part are you struggling with?
Child: I don't know how to formulate a hypothesis or set
up the experiment.
Teacher: Let's brainstorm some ideas together. First,
think about what you want to investigate. Then, we'll
write a hypothesis and plan your experiment.
Conversation 8: Child with Sibling (Conflict Resolution)
•
Situation: Child and sibling fought over a toy.
Child: I didn't like it when you took my toy without asking.
It made me really angry.

[[p.116]]
Sibling: I'm sorry, I should have asked. I didn't mean to
upset you.
Child: Can we agree to ask each other before taking toys?
That would make me feel better.
Sibling: Yes, let's do that. I'm really sorry.
Child: Thanks. I appreciate it.
Conversation 9: Child with Friend (Sharing Feelings)
•
Situation: Child feels lonely and wants to play.
Child: Hey, I've been feeling a bit lonely lately. Do you
want to hang out and play something together?
Friend: Sure, I'd love to. What do you want to play?
Child: Maybe we can play some board games or go outside.
Friend: Sounds fun! Let's do it.
