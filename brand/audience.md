## The book
"Building Tomorrow" by Jitin Khanna: a 16-chapter social-emotional learning (SEL) workbook for ages 11 to 15.

## Track 1: `child_teen` (ages 11 to 15)

**Who they are**
- Reading on their own phone or a shared family one. Instagram is a discovery surface they may be under-age for on paper; we do not target anyone under the platform minimum. `TO CONFIRM`: how to handle this in copy (it is a policy call for the book's owner).
- Scrolling for something that feels true, funny or useful. They leave fast if something feels preachy or adult.

**What they respond to**
- A situation from their own life (friends, school, siblings, pressure, embarrassment, big feelings).
- Naming a feeling they could not name. "Oh, that is a thing?"
- Small, doable actions. One question to try tonight.

**What makes them leave**
- Being told what to feel or do. Adult voice. Long text. Anything that sounds like a school assembly.

## Track 2: `parent_teacher`

**Who they are**
- Parents and teachers of 11 to 15 year olds who want to support emotional growth and are unsure what to say.
- Time-poor, a little worried, tired of parenting content that makes them feel judged.

**What they respond to**
- A concrete script: "Try asking, …" or "Instead of X, try Y".
- Validation that this age is hard for everyone.
- Something they can share with a co-parent or colleague.

**What makes them leave**
- Guilt, blame, jargon, or generic wellbeing advice.

## Cross-track rules
- The two tracks are separate posts, not one post trying to speak to both.
- The book appears the same way in both: as the source of the idea, never the pitch.
- Never expose or imply a real child's identity. Real stories require `consent: true` and are anonymised (see `inbox/real-stories/`).
